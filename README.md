# The Flannan Isles Disappearance

**An interactive, evidence-led forensic case study of the disappearance of three lighthouse keepers from Eilean Mòr, Scotland, in December 1900.**

> *In memory of James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, and Donald McArthur, the three lighthouse keepers who disappeared from Eilean Mòr in December 1900.*
>
> *This investigation is undertaken with respect for their lives, service, and the historical record.*

On 26 December 1900, a relief crew found the Flannan Isles lighthouse unattended. Its three keepers—James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, and Donald McArthur—were missing. This project examines the contemporary reports and physical findings, compares possible event sequences, and demonstrates how probabilistic conclusions depend on assumptions.

## What the site includes

- **Historical background and timeline** for readers unfamiliar with the incident.
- **Evidence register** distinguishing documented observations from interpretation, including the closed doors, outdoor clothing, damage to the western landing, and displaced heavy stone.
- **Operational and geographical constraints**, including the verified practice of rotating night watches and the unresolved question of continuous daytime attendance.
- **Interactive forensic scenarios** comparing joint exposure, attempted assistance, and independent sequential exposure.
- **Assumption testing** for the daytime duty rule, clothing evidence, and the possibility of detecting an emergency.
- **Bayesian sensitivity experiment** with adjustable hypothetical priors and likelihoods.
- **Findings, limitations, source links, and glossary.**

## Important methodological note

The investigation distinguishes **documented observations**, **inferences**, and **hypothetical reconstructions**. The interactive probability outputs are **illustrations of Bayesian updating**, not measured probabilities of what actually happened. The available historical record does not support precise numerical likelihoods for the competing sequences.

The site does not assign blame or infer the men's final intentions or personal conduct. Some records and regulations applicable to the station in 1900 remain unverified.

## Revised forensic model — warning hypothesis

The current version distinguishes **departure sequence** from **fatal exposure timing**, rather than assuming that all three men leaving together follows from all three being lost in one event. Its four selectable, unverified scenarios are joint inspection, attempted warning, attempted rescue, and independent departures. The probability controls have been updated to four hypothetical scenarios and remain sensitivity demonstrations, **not historical probability estimates**. No primary evidence currently documents a warning or rescue attempt.
