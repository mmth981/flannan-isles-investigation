01 / Context

What happened at the lighthouse?

On 26 December 1900, the lighthouse tender Hesperus arrived at Eilean Mòr, one of Scotland's remote Flannan Isles. No keeper responded to the ship's signals. Relief keeper Joseph Moore went ashore and found the station deserted. None of the three men was ever recovered.

James Ducat

Principal lightkeeper

Thomas Marshall

Assistant lightkeeper

Donald McArthur

Occasional keeper, substituting for William Ross

The light appeared to have been prepared for evening operation, but a passing vessel had not observed it around midnight on 15 December. Officials subsequently documented severe damage near the western landing. The incident's precise sequence is unknown. [NRS] [NLB]

Central question. Which proposed event sequence accounts for the lighthouse's condition, differences in outdoor clothing, and damaged western landing while requiring the fewest unsupported assumptions?
02 / Chronology

The documented timeline

7 December 1900

Previous relief visit. The station was operating before the disappearance.

15 December, 9:00 a.m.

Last known weather observations noted on a slate. Morning preparations for the light were completed.

15 December, afternoon — inferred

Superintendent Robert Muirhead later inferred that the men disappeared after the morning work and before evening lightkeeping.

Night of 15 December

The passing ship Archtor reportedly failed to observe the lighthouse light.

26 December

The Hesperus arrived. Moore discovered that the station was deserted.

8 January 1901

Muirhead submitted his investigation, proposing that an unexpectedly large wave had swept the men into the sea.

The date and time of the accident are inferred from available observations, not eyewitness testimony. NRS; NLB.

05 / Interactive forensic reconstruction · revised

Separate departure from fatal exposure

An important revision: leaving the lighthouse together and being lost in the same sea event are different propositions. A keeper might follow the others to warn them before any accident, or to assist after an accident. Neither action is documented.

H1 · Joint inspectionAll three depart togetherOne event
H2 · Attempted warningTwo leave; McArthur follows to warn them before an accidentOne event
H3 · Attempted rescueTwo leave; McArthur responds after an initial incidentSuccessive exposure
H4 · Independent departuresKeepers leave separately for unrelated reasonsOne or more events

        Test the warning hypothesis's feasibility

        Different timing constraints: if McArthur observed a single approaching wave, the time to notice it, depart and travel to the others would have to fit before its arrival. If he instead noticed deteriorating sea conditions, the available warning interval might be much longer. Neither interval is measured.
        T(detection) + T(departure) + T(travel) ≤ T(hazard arrival)

        This inequality applies to a time-specific approaching hazard, not automatically to deteriorating conditions over a longer period.

        Methodological revision log

        Earlier versions treated joint fatal exposure as though it implied joint departure. A subsequent warning hypothesis showed that the two variables must be separated. We therefore replaced the three original sequences with four alternatives and re-ran the illustrative model. No new physical observation was discovered through this revision; the improvement is in hypothesis specification.

        What can be concluded?

        Empirical posterior probabilities for H1–H4Not estimable from the available data
        Consolidated assessment. Destructive marine conditions at the western landing are well documented. A fatal sea-related accident is consistent with the physical and circumstantial evidence; however, joint or sequential departure cannot be established, even if all three were lost in one marine event. The probability calculator is a transparent assumption test rather than a numerical solution to the case.

        Priority sources for further verification

        1. The applicable 1900 Northern Lighthouse Commissioners' regulations, including daytime station-presence requirements.
        2. Original lighthouse plans and landing routes to reconstruct distance and sightlines.
        3. Any surviving watch assignments or local operating instructions for December 1900.
        4. Weather and sea-condition observations near the likely incident period.
        5. Pre-incident inspections recording the state of western landing equipment.

        Archival leads include NLC3/1/1 (incident correspondence) and NLC5/5/4 (an earlier instruction booklet). The relevant 1900 rule has not been authenticated here.

        08 / Reference

        Glossary

        West landing

        A coastal access point on the western side of Eilean Mòr, equipped with steps and mooring infrastructure.

        Occasional keeper

        A lightkeeper appointed temporarily to cover another keeper's absence.

        Night watch

        A rotating period when a keeper attended the operating lighthouse mechanism and light.

        Forensic reconstruction

        A testable proposed sequence of events evaluated against records and physical constraints.

        Departure sequence vs fatal exposure

        Who left the station when is separate from whether the fatal exposure happened in a single event or in successive incidents.

        Prior probability

        Probability assigned to a hypothesis before considering the evidence used in a particular update.

        Likelihood

        The probability of observing particular evidence under a given hypothesis: P(E | H).

        Posterior probability

        The updated probability of a hypothesis after the specified evidence has been incorporated.

        Sensitivity analysis

        Testing whether conclusions change when uncertain model inputs change.

        Wave run-up and backwash

        The movement of wave water up an exposed slope and its subsequent return downslope.

        Correlated evidence

        Observations that share possible causes and should not automatically be multiplied as independent likelihood terms.
